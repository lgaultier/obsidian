Scientific Interest

Remote sensing observation of upper ocean dynamics using multi-sensor data synergy.

Development of scientific simulator for future oceanic satellite mission and production of synthetic satellite data \\My research focuses on the multi-sensor observation of upper ocean dynamics, specifically the transition from mesoscale to submesoscale processes. I specialize in the development of scientific simulators and advanced data assimilation (Lagrangian diagnostics, image assimilation) to reconstruct high-resolution 2D oceanic fields. My goal is to bridge the gap between future wide-swath altimetry missions (like SWOT and S3NG-T) and operational ocean forecasting requirements.

Research background and expected contributions to the S3NGT MAG

Over the past decade, I have developed strong expertise in satellite data simulation and its application to the design, assessment, and exploitation of ocean topography and surface current missions, with a particular focus on altimetry. My research aims to support mission development by providing realistic, physically consistent simulated observations that enable robust performance assessment, algorithm development, and trade-off analysis for future satellite systems such as S3NG-T. A central pillar of my work is the design and use of end-to-end simulation frameworks and Observing System Simulation Experiments (OSSEs). These frameworks combine high-resolution numerical ocean model outputs with realistic representations of satellite sampling, instrument geometry, and measurement errors. Through these simulations, I have contributed to quantifying the added value of wide-swath altimetry, multi-mission constellations, and novel observing concepts for resolving mesoscale and short-mesoscale ocean dynamics. This approach allows the systematic evaluation of how mission parameters—such as swath geometry, orbit configuration, and noise characteristics—impact the observability of key ocean signals. My experience with SWOT-like observations has been particularly relevant to S3NG-T. Using realistic simulated datasets, I have investigated the effects of correlated errors, noise anisotropy, and sampling patterns on sea surface height and surface current reconstruction. These studies have informed algorithm development, mapping strategies, and signal separation methods, including the discrimination between balanced motions and internal tides. I have also extended this simulation-driven methodology to Doppler radar concepts such as SKIM and ODYSEA, enabling cross-mission comparisons and the assessment of synergies between topography and surface current measurements. More recently, I have integrated data-driven and machine-learning approaches within simulation frameworks to test their robustness under realistic observing conditions. Simulated satellite datasets provide controlled environments to assess sensitivity to noise, sampling gaps, and model error, ensuring that algorithmic innovations remain compatible with mission constraints. This combined use of physics-based simulation and data-driven methods is central to my current research activities. Currently at Datlas, I continue to focus on satellite data simulation, validation, and high-level exploitation of ocean observations, with an emphasis on multi-mission synergies and future mission preparation. My work supports the translation of simulation results into actionable guidance for algorithm design and performance evaluation. Within the context of the S3NG-T Phase B2/C activities, my expected contribution is to support estimation studies and algorithm development through realistic satellite data simulation. In particular, I aim to contribute to the assessment of calibration strategies, mapping performance, and signal separability for the dual wide-swath configuration of S3NG-T. Drawing on lessons learned from SWOT and related missions, I intend to help identify potential risks, evaluate design trade-offs, and propose solutions that ensure robust, scientifically valuable S3NG-T topography products.
