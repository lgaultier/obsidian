Research background and expected contributions to the S3NG-T MAG

Over the past decade, I have developed strong expertise in satellite data simulation over the ocean and its application to the design, assessment, and exploitation of ocean topography and surface current missions. Part of my research aims to support mission development by providing realistic, physically consistent simulated observations that enable robust performance assessment, algorithm development, and trade-off analysis for future satellite systems such as S3NG-T.
I have designed open source statistical simulator for the open ocean that are highly scalable and user friendly. It enables scientists to simulate satellite-like data using the ocean model of their choice as a true ocean view. Using the developped simulator, I have also provided support for the following mission by generating gobal, multiyear L2 and L3 like observation: SWOT topography, SKIM doppler radar instrument and its derived concepts, ODYSEA doppler scatterometer, wide swath altimeters such as SWOT or SAOOH. These frameworks combine high-resolution numerical ocean model outputs with realistic representations of satellite sampling, instrument geometry, and measurement errors. Through these simulations, I have contributed to quantifying the added value of wide-swath altimetry, multi-mission constellations, and novel observing concepts for resolving mesoscale and short-mesoscale ocean dynamics. This approach allows the systematic evaluation of how mission parameters (such as swath geometry, orbit configuration, and noise characteristics) impact the observability of key ocean signals.

My experience with SWOT-like observations has been particularly relevant to S3NG-T. Using realistic simulated datasets, I have investigated the effects of correlated errors, noise anisotropy, and sampling patterns on sea surface height and surface current reconstruction. These studies have informed algorithm development, mapping strategies, and signal separation methods. I have also extended this simulation-driven methodology to Doppler concepts such as SKIM and ODYSEA, enabling cross-mission comparisons and the assessment of synergies between topography and surface current measurements.

Currently at OceanDataLab, I continue to focus on satellite data simulation, validation, and high-level exploitation of ocean observations, with an emphasis on multi-mission synergies and future mission preparation. I have also developed innovative algorithms and metrics to compare a wide variety of observation in synergy and assess upper ocean dynamics observation.
Within the context of the S3NG-T Phase B2/C activities, my expected contribution is to support estimation studies and algorithm development through realistic satellite data simulation over the globe and for more than a year. In particular, I aim to contribute to the assessment of the observability of S3NG-T for operationnal application such as data assimilation or the mapping algorithm to generate L4 for different orbit configuration and noise budget. The error budget will also be carfully studied with several algorithm that could be applied on the L2 SSH such as cross calibration techniques to remove systematic errors or mapping of the wet tropospheric measured by the three beams to improve the path delay correction.

I hereby declare that I'm part of a consortium to study the scientific performance of S3NG-T based on inputs from ESA about the instrument performance and the use of SWOT real data. But I'm not involved in any way in any industrial consortium awarded the Phase B2/C/D/E1 industrial contracts run by ESA for this mission.
